# The picture ROM (IC19): the Slide Show's 100 preset pictures

IC19 holds the **100 PRESET pictures** a KN7000 song can show while it plays: landscapes,
cities, Christmas scenes, instruments, animals, title cards and the numbers 1 to 10, each a
640 × 240 JPEG. They are all on this page, read from a real instrument.

> **Status (2026-10-19).** Read through the CPU on a KN7000 (PROGRAM 893 / TABLE 80) with the
> [ROM dumper]({{ site.baseurl }}/kn7000-rom-dumper/)'s IC19 pass. All 100 pictures decode
> cleanly, and they are exactly the 100 the firmware's picture code accepts. The KN7000
> disassembly rebuilds the chip's visible contents byte for byte from these files. Upstream MAME
> still declares IC19 `NO_DUMP`.

## What the pictures are for

The owner's manual calls the feature **Slide Show** (pp. 77, 90–91): "This instrument can show
various images which change automatically corresponding to the music progression just like the
DEMO performance." In STEP RECORD the user marks **PIC points** on a song's control track, each
naming a picture. When the song plays, each picture fills the screen at its point and stays until
the next one ([Slide Shows]({{ site.baseurl }}/kn7000-slideshow/) has the data format).

- A PIC point names either a **PRESET** picture, 0–99, from this ROM, or a **USER** picture, one of
  30 slots filled from a floppy or SD card with PICTURE LOAD.
- The pictures appear on the instrument's display and, through **VIDEO OUT**, on a TV
  (manual p. 199).
- 640 × 240 is the size of every slide-show picture: a USER picture of up to 640 × 480 is cut
  to it when loaded.
- "The preset images are not compatible with other models" (p. 90): these 100 are the KN7000's
  own.

The built-in demonstrations do not use these pictures: their slide shows are made of demo
screens stored in the program and table ROMs (see the
[image gallery]({{ site.baseurl }}/kn7000-image-gallery/)). The title cards (PRESET 80–89) and the
numbers (90–99) look made for a performance: an opening, a dedication, a count, a toast.

## How they are stored

The CPU sees IC19 at `0x57800000`. Its contents:

| offset                | contents                                                                            |
|-----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `0x000000`–`0x000197` | a directory: 102 u32 offsets from `0x57800000`, ascending                           |
| `0x000198`–`0x0003FF` | zero                                                                                |
| `0x000400`            | entry 0: the version string `"7\n"`, shown as `PICTURE : 7` on SOFT VERSION         |
| `0x000404`–`0x1FF49B` | entries 1–100: PRESET 0–99, each a JFIF JPEG padded with zeros to a 4-byte boundary |
| `0x1FF49C`            | entry 101: the end of the data                                                      |
| `0x1FF49C`–`0x1FFFFF` | `0xFF`                                                                              |

The pictures take 2,093,050 bytes: from 6,308 (PRESET 13) to 46,959 (PRESET 84, "Starring The
KN7000!").

Entry 0 is not a picture but the chip's **version**: the bytes `37 0A 00 00`, the ASCII digit
`"7"` and a line feed, the same form as the table flash's `"80\n"`. The hidden
[SOFT VERSION screen]({{ site.baseurl }}/kn7000-soft-version/) prints it as `PICTURE : 7`: its
routine `AcAromVerBoxProc` (`0x48488A0B`) takes the u32 at `0x57800000` (`0x400`), adds the base,
and reads decimal digits from `0x57800400` until the line feed.

## How the firmware finds a picture

A PIC point becomes the picture code `0x8000 + n` for PRESET n. The slide-show screen's routine
(`0x4858099B` in PROGRAM 941) then:

1. checks `n < 100` (`0x48580ABE`–`0x48580AC4`), or shows `NO PICTURE DATA`;
2. reads entry `n + 1` of the directory, `u32[0x57800000 + 4 × (n + 1)]`
   (`0x48580A38`, the firmware's only load of `0x57800000`; `0x4857F783` in PROGRAM 893);
3. decodes the JPEG at `0x57800000` plus that offset.

Nothing selects a bank before the read, so the firmware never looks beyond `0x1FF49C`.

## Why the CPU sees 2 MB of a 64 Mbit chip

The service manual's schematic (p. 102) labels IC19 `C3CBMD000098`, "64M ROM", and shows how it is
wired:

- It runs in 16-bit mode (BYTE tied high), with its data on `D(16)`–`D(31)`.
- CPU address lines `A(1)`–`A(21)` drive its address pins AD0–AD20.
- Its top address pin, **AD21 (pin 12), goes through jumper JP10 to ground**. The CPU can therefore
  reach only half of the chip, 4 MB.

The read shows that half holding the same 2 MB twice. The CPU's 8 MB window,
`0x57800000`–`0x57FFFFFF`, therefore shows one 2 MB image four times, and the four copies are
byte-identical.

The other half of the chip, AD21 high, can be read by no program on the instrument. Only a chip
reader could show what it holds. Emulation does not need it, since the instrument never sees it
either.

## How it was read

The [ROM dumper]({{ site.baseurl }}/kn7000-rom-dumper/)'s IC19 build read `0x57800000`–`0x57FFFFFF`
on the owner's instrument and wrote it to the SD card, 8 MB. The instrument's CRC-32 matched the
file on the computer, and the four 2 MB quarters are identical. The unique 2 MB has CRC-32
`3372cb5c` and SHA-1 `9631dedfc501b8c11bb93efa2d99750e3c84465a`. A second read, to show the reads
are stable, is still to come; the pictures all decoding cleanly already argues for it.

## In the disassembly and in MAME

- **Disassembly.** `kn7000/src/picture.s` in `technics-roms-disasm` declares the directory with labels,
  then brings in each picture as a binary include: `.incbin "src/picture/preset_NN.jpg"`, then
  `.align 4`. `make verify-picture` rebuilds the 2 MB and checks it against the SHA-1 above.
- **MAME.** The driver declares `c3cbmd000098.ic19` as an 8 MB `NO_DUMP` and does not map
  `0x57800000` yet. Since the window is exactly four copies, the plan is to declare the 2 MB image
  and map it with MAME's mirror: `map(0x57800000, 0x579fffff).mirror(0x600000)`.

## Open questions

- Whether that instrument's SOFT VERSION screen shows `PICTURE : 7`, as the chip says it should.
- Whether IC19 differs between production runs. This read comes from an early instrument
  (PROGRAM 893).
- What the unreachable half of the chip holds.
